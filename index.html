How to install it

  1. Show your bookmarks bar if it is hidden. In Chrome or Edge, press Ctrl+Shift+B (Windows) or ⌘+Shift+B (Mac).
  2. Drag the blue button above onto that bar. Don't click it — dragging is what saves it. It will sit there like any other bookmark.
  3. Go to one of your Canvas courses and sign in as you normally would.
  4. Click the Canvas Toolkit bookmark. A menu opens on top of the page; pick the tool you want and it runs right there.

If dragging is awkward, you can also right-click your bookmarks bar, choose Add page, and paste the bookmarklet code from the install page into the URL field.

What's in the toolkit

ToolWhat it does
Syllabus Auto-Builder Reads your published modules, assignments and grading groups and builds a syllabus summary — weekly schedule, assignment list, grading breakdown, and a list of anything still unpublished. Downloads as a Word file you can edit.
Class Roster Builder Builds a printable roster of the students in the course, with contact and last-activity information pulled straight from Canvas.
At-Risk Early Warning Dashboard Flags students whose grades, submissions or participation suggest they are falling behind, so you can reach out while it still matters.
Peer Comparison Snapshot Shows how one student's activity and scores compare with the rest of the section, without you having to pull the gradebook apart.
Re-Engagement Email Drafter Drafts personalized check-in messages to students who have gone quiet, ready for you to review, edit and send.
Weekly Announcement Auto-Drafter Drafts a weekly announcement from what is actually due that week in your course, so you start from a draft instead of a blank box.
Discussion to Word Exporter Exports a discussion thread — posts and replies, in order — to a Word document for grading, archiving or accreditation files.
Engagement Report Summarizes participation across the course: who is active, who is drifting, and how that has changed over time.

What it can and can't see

Every tool runs in your own browser, signed in as you, and asks Canvas for the same information Canvas already shows you. Nothing is sent to an outside server, and the toolkit can never see a course you don't have access to.

  • Run it from inside a course. Most tools read the course ID out of the page address, so start from a course page rather than your dashboard.
  • Large courses may be trimmed. Canvas returns at most 100 items per request, so very large courses can need a manual look at anything past that.
  • Drafts are drafts. The email and announcement tools write text for you to review — they do not send or post anything on their own.
  • Student data stays student data. Rosters, grades and engagement reports are protected records. Treat anything you export the way you would treat a printed gradebook.

If something doesn't work

  • Nothing happens when you click the bookmark. Make sure you are on a Canvas page and signed in, then reload the page and try again.
  • The menu opens but a tool reports an error. Check that you are inside a course — the address should contain /courses/ and a number.
  • The bookmark saved as a normal web page. Delete it and drag the blue button again; a bookmarklet's address starts with javascript: rather than https:.
